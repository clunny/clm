## ADDED Requirements

### Requirement: push-turn
Every `clm land` attempt SHALL hold the clone's *push turn*, which one attempt holds at a time, from before the fetch of `main` its push relies on until that push has gone through or the attempt ends, on every exit path, so that between that fetch and that push no other `clm land` of the clone moves `main`. An attempt that runs `[land] check` on its merge SHALL take the turn once that check has passed, still holding the land lock (`land-target-lock`), and SHALL run inside it whatever `overlap-recheck` then asks. An attempt that runs no `[land] check` SHALL take the push turn and no other lock, so it never waits for a check that isn't an overlap re-check. The turn SHALL go to the attempts waiting for it in the order they asked, and an attempt that waits SHALL print `waiting for another land's push: <n> ahead` on stderr when it begins to wait and each time `<n>` changes.

#### Scenario: a land with nothing to check doesn't wait for a check
- GIVEN a branch `impl/a` with no `checked` record, a `[land] check` that waits for a file to appear, and a branch whose head changes only `specs/x/SPEC.md`, whose merged tree's code key has a `checked` record and whose readers select nothing
- WHEN `clm land impl/a` is in its check and `clm land` of the other branch runs
- THEN the other branch lands before the file appears, and its stderr holds no `waiting` line

#### Scenario: two lands with nothing to check
- GIVEN two branches whose lands run no `[land] check`, and a fake `CLM_CHECK` that sleeps 2 seconds and passes
- WHEN `clm land` starts for both at once
- THEN both land, the one that waited printed `waiting for another land's push: 1 ahead`, and neither's stderr holds a `main moved` line

#### Scenario: turns in the order asked
- GIVEN three branches whose lands run no `[land] check`, and a fake `CLM_CHECK` that sleeps 2 seconds and passes
- WHEN `clm land` starts for the first, then the second once the first holds the turn, then the third once the second has printed its waiting line
- THEN `main`'s first-parent history holds their merge commits in that order

#### Scenario: released when the attempt fails
- GIVEN a branch whose land runs no `[land] check` and a fake `CLM_CHECK` that exits 1
- WHEN `clm land` runs for it and then for a second such branch with a `CLM_CHECK` that passes
- THEN the first exits 1, and the second lands and prints no `waiting` line

#### Serves
- capability/work-concurrently
- capability/land-safely

### Requirement: batched-lands
A `clm land` that takes the land lock SHALL land with its own branch, on one run of `[land] check`, the other lands of the clone that are waiting for that lock and whose tickets name their branch, in the order their turns would have come (`lock-queue`); a waiting land whose ticket names none, as an older `clm`'s, SHALL be left to wait. For each land it takes it SHALL do what that land's own attempt does before its check, and SHALL run the gate on that land's own merge commit as a land runs it on its merge. It SHALL leave out, to wait as before, a land either of those would refuse, one that conflicts with `main` and the lands taken before it, and one whose check environment differs from its own (`check-env`). The lands taken SHALL reach `main` in one push, each as its own merge commit `Merge <branch> into main` in that order, with `[land] check` and `clm check` run once, on the last of those commits, the check's selection holding every test one of them would have run landing alone and every test two of them can both break. Each land taken SHALL get the `effort` and `landed` records its own land would have appended, except that its `landed` record names, with its own merge commit, the code key the check ran on, where a land alone names its merged tree's (`land`); says `recheck: selected` with the shared selection's `recheck_files`; and carries the check's `check_seconds` and `steps` only on the record of the land that held the lock (`step-times`). So `clm ledger verify` finds each of them recorded and checked. Each waiting `clm land` SHALL then print `landed <its merge commit> <kind>`, exit 0 and remove what a land removes, having run no `[land] check` itself. A land with no other waiting SHALL land as before this rule. The capability test `crates/clm/tests/capability/land_safely.rs`'s `a_branch_that_breaks_main` and `slow-tests-after-land`'s land over its target land alone, and SHALL still observe the check and the timing they assert.

#### Scenario: three waiting lands, one check
- GIVEN branches `impl/a`, `impl/b` and `impl/c` on unrelated components with no `checked` records, a `[land] check` that appends `start` to a log outside the repository and sleeps 2 seconds, and another process holding the land lock
- WHEN `clm land` starts for `impl/a`, then `impl/b`, then `impl/c`, each once the one before has printed its waiting line, and the holder releases the lock
- THEN the log holds one `start`, `main`'s first-parent history ends with `Merge impl/a into main`, `Merge impl/b into main` and `Merge impl/c into main`, each `clm land` printed `landed` with its own commit, the ledger holds a `landed` record for each with the same `code_key`, only `impl/a`'s holds `check_seconds`, and `clm ledger verify` prints nothing

#### Scenario: a land that conflicts with one before it
- GIVEN the same, with `impl/b` and `impl/c` both changing the same line of a file `main` holds
- WHEN the three lands run as above
- THEN `impl/a` and `impl/b` land on one check, and `impl/c`'s `clm land` exits 1 telling the caller to run `clm rebase`, as it does landing alone

#### Scenario: a land the gate refuses once another is merged before it
- GIVEN `impl/a` and `spec/b` waiting in that order, and a fake `CLM_GATE` that passes except on a merge commit of `spec/b` whose first parent holds `impl/a`, where it reports `gate/approval`
- WHEN the holder releases the lock
- THEN `impl/a` lands alone on its check, and `spec/b` is left to wait

#### Scenario: the selection covers what two of them share
- GIVEN components `a` and `b`, a component `c` with `depends_on: [a, b]` and a test `crates/c/tests/t.rs`, branches `impl/a` and `impl/b` each with a `checked` record for its head, and a `[land] check` that copies the file `CLM_SELECTION` names
- WHEN the two lands wait and are landed together
- THEN the copy holds `file crates/c/tests/t.rs`

#### Scenario: a ticket that names no branch
- GIVEN `impl/a` waiting, and a second waiting ticket that names no branch
- WHEN the holder releases the lock
- THEN `impl/a` lands alone, and the second ticket is still waiting

#### Scenario: a land alone
- GIVEN a branch with no `checked` record and nothing else waiting
- WHEN `clm land` runs
- THEN it lands as before, with one merge commit and one `landed` record holding `check_seconds`

#### Serves
- capability/work-concurrently
- capability/land-safely

### Requirement: failed-batch
When `[land] check` or `clm check` fails on lands taken together (`batched-lands`), the land that holds the lock SHALL push nothing, and SHALL say nothing of the failure to the others. The *suspects* are the lands taken whose own part of the selection, what each would have run landing alone and what it and another of them can both break, holds a path the step that was open as the check's output ended named with a `::clm-selected::` line (`check-log`, `step-times`); when no step was open, the step named no path, or it was `clm check` that failed, every land taken is a suspect. The holder SHALL then, before it releases the lock:
- take together again the lands that aren't suspects, when there are any, and treat a failure of theirs as this rule says;
- when there are four suspects or fewer, leave each to land alone, as a land alone lands or fails, with that land's own output, log and exit code: its own at once, and each other on its own turn, when it SHALL neither take other lands with it nor be taken with another's;
- when there are more than four, take together the first half of them, then the second, each half as this rule says.

So a `clm land` is told its check failed only by its own run, on a tree holding `main` and its branch alone.

#### Scenario: the failed step points at one land
- GIVEN three waiting lands as in `batched-lands`' first scenario, where `impl/c` makes `[land] check` exit 1 inside a step that printed `::clm-selected::crates/c/tests/t.rs`, a path only `impl/c`'s selection holds
- WHEN the holder releases the lock
- THEN `impl/a` and `impl/b` land on the second run of the check, and `impl/c`'s `clm land` then runs the check alone, exits 1 naming the check command, and `main` doesn't hold `impl/c`

#### Scenario: no step says who
- GIVEN the same three lands, a `[land] check` that marks no steps, and `impl/c` making it exit 1
- WHEN the holder releases the lock
- THEN the check runs four times in all, once on the three together and once on each alone, `impl/a` and `impl/b` land, and `impl/c`'s `clm land` exits 1 naming the check command

#### Scenario: the holder's own branch breaks the check
- GIVEN the same, where `impl/a` makes the check exit 1
- WHEN the holder releases the lock
- THEN `impl/a`'s `clm land` exits 1 naming the check command after running the check alone, and `impl/b` and `impl/c` then land, each alone

#### Scenario: more than four suspects are halved
- GIVEN six waiting lands `impl/a` to `impl/f` on unrelated components, a `[land] check` that marks no steps and appends `start` to a log, and `impl/f` making it exit 1
- WHEN the holder releases the lock
- THEN `impl/a`, `impl/b` and `impl/c` land together on the second run, `impl/d` and `impl/e` each land alone, `impl/f`'s `clm land` exits 1, and the log holds six `start` lines

#### Serves
- capability/work-concurrently
- capability/land-safely

### Requirement: unrecorded-land-finished
A `clm land` of a branch whose head `main` already holds, merged by a commit on `main`'s first-parent chain that no `landed` record names, as a land killed between its push and its records leaves it, SHALL finish that land and merge nothing. When a `checked` record covers the commit, naming its own code key or, for a commit pushed with lands taken together (`batched-lands`), the code key their check ran on, and the land was one `clm land` would have made, judged on that commit as a land judges its merge (the branch's fingerprint taken from the commit's two parents and approved under the policy at its first parent, and the gate run on the commit), it SHALL append the `landed` record naming that commit and that key, with no `recheck`, `check_seconds` or `steps`, and the branch's `effort` records, print `landed <commit> <kind>` and exit 0. It SHALL do the same for every other commit of that push whose `landed` record is missing, so that after one of the lands taken together has run, `clm ledger verify` reports none of them. When no `checked` record covers the commit, or the land was not one `clm land` would have made, it SHALL append nothing and exit 1 naming the commit and `ledger/unrecorded-land`.

#### Scenario: lands taken together, pushed and not recorded
- GIVEN `main` holding the merge commits of three lands taken together as `batched-lands` pushes them, a `checked` record for the code key their check ran on, and no `landed` record for any of them
- WHEN `clm land impl/b` runs
- THEN it prints `landed` with `impl/b`'s merge commit and exits 0, `main` is unchanged, the ledger holds a `landed` record for each of the three commits, and `clm ledger verify` prints nothing

#### Scenario: a land alone, pushed and not recorded
- GIVEN `main` holding the merge commit of `impl/a` as a land alone pushes it, a `checked` record for that commit's code key, and no `landed` record for it
- WHEN `clm land impl/a` runs
- THEN it prints `landed` with that commit, `main` is unchanged, and `clm ledger verify` prints nothing

#### Scenario: a merge no land made
- GIVEN a commit pushed to `main` by hand that merges `impl/a`, whose code key no `checked` record names
- WHEN `clm land impl/a` runs
- THEN it exits 1 naming the commit and `ledger/unrecorded-land`, and the ledger is unchanged

#### Scenario: an unapproved branch pushed by hand
- GIVEN a branch that needs review and has no approval, and a commit pushed to `main` by hand that merges it, whose code key a `checked` record names
- WHEN `clm land` runs for the branch
- THEN it exits 1 naming the commit and `ledger/unrecorded-land`, and the ledger is unchanged

#### Serves
- capability/land-safely
- architecture/git-is-truth

### Requirement: land-target-lock <!-- base: 66ccebd095374496 -->
A `clm land` that will run `[land] check` SHALL hold an exclusive advisory lock on `.clm/land.lock` beside the main worktree's `.clm/land` from before it checks the head out into the land tree until the push of the merge commit has gone through and the target's cap has been applied (`land-target-cap`), or the attempt ends sooner, so that the gate on the merge commit, `clm check`, the push and the cap run under it, releasing it on every exit path, whatever `CARGO_TARGET_DIR` the caller set. A land that waits for the lock SHALL say so on stderr, naming `.clm/land`. A land that waited for the lock SHALL merge its branch with `main` as `origin` holds it once the lock is its own, never with a `main` it fetched before it waited, whichever check it then runs. A land that runs no `[land] check`, because its code key was already checked or no check is set, SHALL NOT take this lock.

#### Scenario: overlapping lands
- GIVEN two branches `impl/a` and `impl/b` of one clone, no `checked` records, and a `[land] check` that appends `start <branch>` to a log, sleeps 2 seconds and appends `end <branch>`
- WHEN `clm land impl/a` and `clm land impl/b` start at once, from two linked worktrees
- THEN in the log every `start <branch>` is followed by the `end` of that same branch before any other `start`, so the two checks never interleave, and the land that waited printed `waiting` naming `.clm/land` on stderr

#### Scenario: a caller's target takes the lock too
- GIVEN the same branches and check, and `CARGO_TARGET_DIR` set to a different directory of the caller's for each land
- WHEN `clm land impl/a` and `clm land impl/b` start at once
- THEN both land, the two checks never interleave in the log, and the land that waited printed `waiting` naming `.clm/land` on stderr

#### Scenario: held through the gate on the merge
- GIVEN the same two branches, a `[land] check` that appends `check <branch>` to a log and sleeps 2 seconds, and a fake `CLM_GATE` that appends `gate` to it and passes
- WHEN `clm land impl/a` and `clm land impl/b` start at once
- THEN the log reads `gate`, `check <one branch>`, `gate` and then `gate`, `check <the other>`, `gate`: both of a land's gate runs fall between its lock's taking and release, never inside the other land's

#### Scenario: checked code takes no lock
- GIVEN a branch that changes only `specs/x/SPEC.md`, a `checked` record for `origin/main`'s code key, and another process holding the lock
- WHEN `clm land` runs
- THEN it lands without waiting and prints no `waiting` line

#### Scenario: a land that waited checks the new main
- GIVEN a branch `impl/a` with no `checked` record, a branch `spec/x` whose merged tree's code key has a `checked` record and whose readers select a test, and a `[land] check` that appends to a log the files of the tree it runs in and sleeps 2 seconds
- WHEN `clm land impl/a` starts, and `clm land spec/x` starts once `impl/a` holds the lock
- THEN both land, `spec/x`'s check ran in a tree holding `impl/a`'s file, and `spec/x`'s stderr holds no `main moved` line

#### Scenario: lock released after a failing check
- GIVEN a `[land] check` that exits 1
- WHEN `clm land` runs and then a second `clm land` runs
- THEN the first exits 1 and the second doesn't print `waiting`

### Requirement: lock-queue <!-- base: 7af00d9536501122 -->
The clone's land lock, `.clm/land.lock` (`land-target-lock`), and its check lock, `.clm/check.lock` (`check-lock`), SHALL each go to the runs waiting for it in an order CLM sets, not in the operating system's: a run that must wait takes a ticket in a queue beside the lock in the main worktree's `.clm/`, and tries the lock only when its turn comes. A ticket SHALL carry a *lane*, priority or ordinary, and a priority from `P1` to `P5`. A `clm land` takes a priority-lane ticket when its merged tree's code key counts as checked and its readers select a test (`selected-tests`), so that its `[land] check` runs only those readers, and an ordinary one otherwise, a land whose readers' selection failed, so that every test runs, included; its priority is `orchestrator`'s `land_priority` for the branch (`orchestrator/land-priority`), `P5` when that can't be computed. Every other run takes an ordinary ticket at `P5`. The turn SHALL go first to the `P1` tickets, a priority-lane one before an ordinary one and otherwise the one taken first; then to the other priority-lane tickets, the highest priority first and then the one taken first; then to the other ordinary tickets in the same order. When six runs in a row below `P1` have taken the lock with priority-lane tickets while an ordinary ticket was waiting, and no `P1` ticket is waiting, the ordinary ticket whose turn would come first among the ordinary ones SHALL take the next turn. A ticket SHALL hold no turn once its run has ended, however it ended, and no ticket of an ended run SHALL stay in the queue past the next run that reads it. A waiting run SHALL print its waiting line on stderr when it begins to wait and again each time the count changes, ending `: <n> ahead`, `<n>` counting the run that holds the lock and the waiting runs whose turn comes before its own. A run that finds the lock free and no ticket waiting SHALL take the lock at once, printing nothing. The order SHALL decide only which run takes the lock next: a run that takes the lock without the queue, such as one by an older `clm`, still never holds it beside another. The push turn has an order of its own (`push-turn`).

#### Scenario: turns in arrival order
- GIVEN another process holding `.clm/check.lock`, three linked worktrees `a`, `b` and `c` on branches no place names, a `[land] check` that appends `start <worktree>` to a log outside them, and a fake `CLM_CHECK` that passes
- WHEN `clm land --check-only` starts in `a`, then in `b` once `a` has printed its waiting line, then in `c` once `b` has, and then the holder releases the lock
- THEN the log reads `start a`, `start b`, `start c`, and `c` printed a waiting line ending `: 3 ahead`, then `: 2 ahead`, then `: 1 ahead`

#### Scenario: a checked land goes first
- GIVEN another process holding `.clm/check.lock`, a `clm land --check-only` run outside any build place waiting for it, and then a `clm land` of a branch that changes only a file a test reads, whose merged tree's code key has a `checked` record and whose readers select that test
- WHEN the holder releases the lock
- THEN the land's check starts before the `--check-only` run's, and the land printed a waiting line ending `: 1 ahead`

#### Scenario: priority runs can't starve the rest
- GIVEN another process holding the lock, one run waiting with an ordinary `P5` ticket, and then eight runs waiting with priority-lane `P5` tickets
- WHEN the holder releases the lock and each run releases it as soon as it holds it
- THEN the lock goes to six priority-lane runs, then the ordinary run, then the other two priority-lane runs

#### Scenario: a killed waiter holds no turn
- GIVEN another process holding `.clm/check.lock`, a `clm land --check-only` run outside any build place waiting for it, and a second such run waiting after it
- WHEN the first waiting run is killed with `SIGKILL`, and then the holder releases the lock
- THEN the second run's waiting line goes from `: 2 ahead` to `: 1 ahead`, it takes the lock and passes, and its queue holds no ticket once it has ended

#### Scenario: nothing waiting
- GIVEN no process holding `.clm/check.lock` and an empty queue
- WHEN `clm land --check-only` runs outside any build place
- THEN it prints no waiting line, and the queue holds no ticket once it has ended

#### Scenario: a run outside the queue
- GIVEN a process holding `.clm/check.lock` with no ticket, as an older `clm` would, and a `clm land --check-only` run waiting for it
- WHEN the waiting run's turn comes while the process still holds the lock
- THEN the run waits until the process releases it, its waiting line counting the process as `1 ahead`, and the two never hold the lock at once

#### Scenario: P1 goes first
- GIVEN another process holding the lock, a run waiting with a priority-lane `P5` ticket, then a run with an ordinary `P1` ticket, then a run with a priority-lane `P1` ticket
- WHEN the holder releases the lock and each run releases it as soon as it holds it
- THEN the lock goes to the priority-lane `P1` run, then the ordinary `P1` run, then the priority-lane `P5` run

#### Scenario: below P1, cheap before urgent
- GIVEN another process holding the lock, a run waiting with an ordinary `P2` ticket, then a run with a priority-lane `P4` ticket, then a run with an ordinary `P3` ticket, then a run with a priority-lane `P3` ticket
- WHEN the holder releases the lock and each run releases it as soon as it holds it
- THEN the lock goes to the priority-lane `P3` run, the priority-lane `P4` run, the ordinary `P2` run, then the ordinary `P3` run

#### Scenario: P1 doesn't wait for the cap
- GIVEN another process holding the lock, an ordinary `P5` ticket waiting, six runs below `P1` having just taken the lock in a row with priority-lane tickets while it waited, and a run waiting with an ordinary `P1` ticket
- WHEN the holder releases the lock
- THEN the `P1` run takes it before the ordinary `P5` run

#### Scenario: a P1 land
- GIVEN another process holding `.clm/check.lock`, a `clm land --check-only` run outside any build place waiting for it, and then a `clm land` of `impl/a` whose code key has no `checked` record, where `a`'s effective priority on `origin/main` is `P1`
- WHEN the holder releases the lock
- THEN the land's check starts before the `--check-only` run's, and the land printed a waiting line ending `: 1 ahead`

### Requirement: overlap-recheck <!-- base: 272fc09e93159f6b -->
A `clm land` attempt whose merged tree's code key no `checked` record names with the land's command, directly or through the settings-free key (`settings-unchanged-is-checked`), SHALL check only the overlap when all of these hold: `[land] check` is set; a `checked` record with the same command names the code key of the branch's head `H`; one names the code key of the fetched base `B`; and `H` doesn't contain `B`. It SHALL run `clm affected --overlap <H> <B>` (`check.affected/overlap-selection`) in the tree on the merge commit, and then:
- when it prints nothing, run no `[land] check`, take neither the lock on `.clm/land.lock` nor the one on `.clm/check.lock`, run `clm check` with its detectors on the merge commit in a temporary worktree as an attempt with no lock does, and print `re-check: none, the branch and main since <short merge base> affect no test in common` on stderr;
- when it prints a line, go on as an attempt whose code key isn't checked, with `CLM_SELECTION` naming a file holding that output unchanged in place of `selected-tests`' selection, and print `re-check: overlap, <n> files` on stderr, `<n>` the number of its `file` lines, or `re-check: overlap, every test` when it prints an `all` line;
- when it fails, print `clm affected failed: <the first non-empty line of its stderr, or the spawn error>; the land checks its own selection` and go on as `selected-tests` says.

A check that passed SHALL NOT be run again because `main` moved. An attempt that follows one of the same `clm land` whose `[land] check` and `clm check` passed on its merge commit `P` with an earlier `main`, whichever path that attempt took, the readers of checked code included, SHALL take `P` for `H` when a `checked` record with the land's command names the code key of the fetched base `B`: it SHALL run `clm affected --overlap <P> <B>` and go on as the three cases above say, inside its push turn (`push-turn`), so that it runs again neither its own selection nor its readers, only what its changes and `main`'s new commits can both break. Its composed `checked` record's `basis` SHALL name `P`'s code key and `B`'s; when the new merged tree's code key already counts as checked, it SHALL append none.

When `clm check` and any `[land] check` it ran pass, the land SHALL append a `checked` record for the merged tree's code key with the land's command and a `basis` naming the two code keys (`ledger/composed-check`), and its `landed` record SHALL say how it re-checked (`ledger/landed-recheck`): `recheck: none`, or `overlap` with `recheck_files` the number of `file` lines, null for an `all` line. Every other land SHALL say it too: `checked` when its code key counted as checked, and `selected` when it ran `selected-tests`' selection, with `recheck_files` counted the same way, null when every test ran. The gate on the merge commit and the push SHALL run as for any land. An attempt for which any condition fails SHALL run as before this rule. The capability tests whose lands must run a check, `crates/clm/tests/capability/land_safely.rs`'s `a_branch_that_breaks_main` and `slow-tests-after-land`'s land over its target, SHALL still observe the check they assert: this rule's build checks that their fixtures make the overlap select the test they break, or gives them a component graph that does.

#### Scenario: main moved on an unrelated component
- GIVEN components `a` and `b` with no `depends_on` between them, a `[land] check` that appends to a log outside the repository, a branch `impl/a` whose head changes only `crates/a/src/lib.rs` and has a `checked` record, and `main` moved since by a land changing only `crates/b/src/lib.rs`, whose code key has a `checked` record
- WHEN `clm land impl/a` runs while another process holds the lock on `.clm/check.lock`
- THEN it prints `landed`, without waiting, the log is empty, stderr holds `re-check: none`, and the ledger has a `checked` record for the merged tree's code key whose `basis` names both keys, and the `landed` record holds `recheck: none`

#### Scenario: a dependant of both
- GIVEN the same, and a component `c` with `depends_on: [a, b]` and a test `crates/c/tests/t.rs`, and a `[land] check` that copies the file `CLM_SELECTION` names
- WHEN `clm land impl/a` runs
- THEN the copy holds `file crates/c/tests/t.rs` and no file of `a` or `b`, stderr holds `re-check: overlap, 1 files`, and the `landed` record holds `recheck: overlap` and `recheck_files: 1`

#### Scenario: the head was never checked whole
- GIVEN the first scenario's repository with no `checked` record for the head
- WHEN `clm land impl/a` runs
- THEN the check runs with `clm affected --base`'s selection, as `selected-tests` says, and stderr holds no `re-check:` line

#### Scenario: main's head not checked
- GIVEN the first scenario's repository, with `main`'s head a commit pushed outside `clm land`, whose code key no `checked` record names
- WHEN `clm land impl/a` runs
- THEN the check runs with `clm affected --base`'s selection and stderr holds no `re-check:` line

#### Scenario: main moved during the check, on an unrelated component
- GIVEN components `a` and `b` with no `depends_on` between them, a branch `impl/a` with no `checked` record, a `[land] check` that appends `start` to a log outside the repository and waits for a file to appear, and a branch whose head changes only `crates/b/src/lib.rs` and whose land runs no check
- WHEN `clm land impl/a` is in its check, the other branch lands, and the file appears
- THEN `impl/a` lands, the log holds one `start`, its stderr holds `re-check: none`, and the `checked` record for the tree it landed has a `basis` naming the code key of the merge commit it checked and that of the `main` it landed on

#### Scenario: main moved during a readers' check
- GIVEN a branch `spec/x` whose merged tree's code key has a `checked` record and whose readers select test `t`, the same waiting `[land] check`, and a branch whose land runs no check and changes no file `t` reads
- WHEN `clm land spec/x` is in its check, the other branch lands, and the file appears
- THEN `spec/x` lands, and the log holds one `start`

#### Scenario: main moved during the check, with a dependant in common
- GIVEN the first of these scenarios, a component `c` with `depends_on: [a, b]` and a test `crates/c/tests/t.rs`, and a `[land] check` that also copies the file `CLM_SELECTION` names on each run
- WHEN `clm land impl/a` is in its check, the other branch lands, and the file appears
- THEN `impl/a` lands, its check ran twice, and the second copy holds `file crates/c/tests/t.rs` and no file of `a` or `b`

## MODIFIED Contract

Landing a branch on `main`, on the machine that runs the command (`land-locally`). `clm land [<branch>]` runs the gate against `origin/main`, merges the branch in a scratch tree, runs the project's check command, handing it the tests the change can break as `clm affected` selects them (`CLM_SELECTION`, `graph-test-selection`), and `clm check` on the result unless the ledger says that code was already checked, records what it did in the ledger, and pushes the merge commit to `main` without force, retrying when `main` moved. It is the only thing in CLM that pushes `main`. What it keeps in a clone, the land tree, the shared target `.clm/land`, its lock, the check lock `.clm/check.lock`, the push turn's lock, the three locks' queues and failing checks' logs, is under the `.clm/` of the main worktree's CLM root, which `clm init` has the repository ignore, so a land leaves nothing untracked whatever the project's language (`land-places-ignored`). It prints `landed <sha> <kind>`, `<kind>` being `code`, `spec` or `spec review` as the gate's first line gives it, and exits 0; it exits 1 with the reason when it pushed nothing, and 2 on a git failure. Whether the branch is approved or held by a request for changes, and the signing of approvals and lands, are `land.approval`'s, as is `clm approve`, which `clm-land` re-exports. There is no after-merge review: a land that needs review lands only once approved (`yes-no-review`). `clm reconnect` pushes `main` and the local ledgers to an `origin` that `main` fast-forwards, as when a repository with `host = "none"` is moved onto a real remote or one landed with no remote by an older `clm` gets one. `clm land --check-only` runs the same check command, with every test (no `CLM_SELECTION`), and `clm check` on the current working tree, so a session learns the land check's verdict through a command that names no path (`land-check-command`). It lands nothing; when both pass on a clean working tree whose committed `HEAD` contains `origin/main`, it appends a `checked` record for `HEAD`'s code key, so a land whose merged tree is that tree, `main` not having moved, runs no check of its own (`worktree-check-counts`). The check command of a land, and of a `--check-only` run outside a build place, waits for any other such check of the same clone to end first (`check-runs-one-at-a-time`), so either can take as long as the checks queued before it; the waiting runs take their turns in the order they began to wait, except that a land carrying `P1` work goes first, a land whose code is already checked and runs only the tests that read what it changed goes next, and the rest go by priority (`lock-queue`, `ordered-lock-queue`); a `--check-only` run in a build place waits for none, its tests kept apart from other runs' by its slot (`tests-run-concurrently`). Both run it with `CLM_SLOT` set to the slot of the build place that names the branch checked, as `orchestrator`'s `place_slot` gives it, or 0, so the project's tests key their ports and paths apart from those of the tests other builds run at the same time (`build-slots`, `tests-run-concurrently`). While `origin/main`'s backlog holds a `kind: bug` item with `verify_failures`, a slow test the human took in as broken after the land, `clm land` refuses a code land that changes a file of one of the item's components, unless a commit of the branch resolves that item or another item holding the same component, so separate fixes for items that hold one component each land; it warns, and lands, a branch touching the components of a red `verified` result no item has taken in yet (`verify-stop`, `verify-after-land`). A host's run never holds a land by itself (`land-locally`). `clm-land` also offers the runner it runs `[land] check` through, under the clone's check lock, for `verify` to run `[verify] check` the same way (`shared-check-runner`), and the cap it applies to `.clm/land`, for `verify` to bound its own build place the same way (`shared-target-cap`). When a land runs the check command it times the command and each step the check marks, and keeps the times, with the files that selected each step, on the `landed` record it appends (`step-times`); `--check-only` times nothing. After the push of such a land it prints the check's time against `[land] check_target_seconds` and, over it, its costliest steps, the chain that selected each and how recent lands fared, so an agent can judge whether the selection reaches too far or a suite belongs after the land (`check-time-report`); it files nothing, and the report never changes the land's outcome. Every land takes the clone's push turn around its push, printing `waiting for another land's push: <n> ahead` on stderr when it waits (`push-turn`), and a land whose check passed runs again, when `main` has moved, only what its changes and `main`'s new commits can both break (`overlap-recheck`). Lands waiting for the land lock are landed together on one check by the one that takes it; each still gets its own merge commit and `landed` record and its `clm land` prints the same `landed` line, so not every commit of `main` is a tree a check ran on, though every commit a push left `main` at is (`batched-lands`, `failed-batch`). A `clm land` of a branch `main` already holds through a merge no `landed` record names finishes that land's records and merges nothing (`unrecorded-land-finished`).

## MODIFIED Design

Crate `clm-land`, called from `crates/clm`'s `land`, `approve` (re-exported from `clm-approval`) and `reconnect` subcommands. Every git call goes through `clm-git`, the remote's name taken from `REMOTE` (`git-plumbing-layer`).

**`clm land [<branch>]`**, default the current branch, refused on `main`. Each attempt, up to ten:
1. Fetch `origin` (`main`, the branch, `clm/ledger/*`) and fold the ledger. Take the head, its fingerprint and change IDs from `land.approval`. In a signed repository its key check comes first, then its hold check, then whether the fingerprint is approved under the policy at the fetched base: all before the gate, the lock, the merge and the checks.
2. Run the gate on the head against `origin/main` in the tree this attempt checks in (**Places**, below), adding `--approved` when approved; for a head **Behind in `specs/`** (below), on the merge commit instead, once step 4 has made it. A violation prints as the gate printed it and exits 1; `gate/approval` tells the caller to ask the human for `clm approve <branch>`.
3. `git merge-tree --write-tree origin/main <head>`. A conflict, or `specs/` changed on both sides, exits 1 telling the caller to run `clm rebase`, except for a head **Behind in `specs/`** (below), whose merged `specs/` the land regenerates.
4. The merge commit, `git commit-tree <merged tree> -p origin/main -p <head>`, message `Merge <branch> into main`, with `Change: <id>` on a following line for a spec land that adds or modifies live changes (the first by ID), committed as the repository's git user. A head that already contains `main` is still merged with a merge commit, so a claim's tip reaches `main` only through its land (`merged-claims-released`). The commit is checked out detached into the tree, so the gate and the checks run on a commit. Then **Derived specs** (below).
5. The merged tree's code key (`ledger`). When a `checked` record names it with the same command, it asks **Readers of checked code** (below): when they select no file, only `clm check --no-detectors` runs; when they select some, `[land] check` runs as below with their selection and then `clm check --no-detectors`, and no second `checked` record is appended. When none names it and the CLM root's `clm.toml` differs between the fetched base and the merge commit only in what `clm-gate`'s `settings_unchanged` leaves out (`upgraded`, `declined_migrations`, comments and formatting), it looks up the *settings-free key* instead, in the same place the attempt computes the code key, before the lock (**Places**), so a land it counts as checked takes no lock unless its readers select a file, `ledger`'s `code_key_with(root, <merge>, <prefix>clm.toml, <base>)`: when a `checked` record names that with the same command, the land goes on as one whose code key is checked, as above, and once `clm check --no-detectors` (and the readers' check, when they selected a file) passes it appends a `checked` record for the merged tree's own code key, so the `landed` record names a checked key and a retry finds it (`settings-unchanged-is-checked`); otherwise **Selected tests** (below), `[land] check` through `sh -c` with `CI` unset (so a script that installs a browser only in CI doesn't), `CLM_LAND_DIR` set to `<home>/.clm/land` (`land-dir-env`), and `CARGO_TARGET_DIR` and `CARGO_INCREMENTAL` left as the caller has them, since where a build's output goes is the project's check script's choice (`shared-land-target`), then `clm check`, and on success a `checked` record. `<main>` is the first worktree `git worktree list --porcelain` lists and `<home>` the CLM root in it, `<main>/<prefix>` with `<prefix>` what `git rev-parse --show-prefix` prints in the CLM root, computed once; a land from a build's worktree under `.claude/worktrees/` so shares one target (`build-places`). The gate then runs again on the merge commit, since a file both sides touched can change whether it needs review, and in a signed repository `land.approval` signs the `landed` record.
6. `git push origin <merge>:refs/heads/main --force-with-lease=refs/heads/main:<the base step 1 fetched>`. A refusal isn't always `main` moving (`land-push-retry`), so it fetches `origin`'s `main` again, directly rather than through step 1's offline refusal, and judges it: the merge commit means the push went through; neither the merge commit nor the base means `main` moved and the next attempt starts, its `checked` record standing, so a retry with the same code key runs only `clm check --no-detectors`; still the base means a refusal for another reason, such as `[remote rejected] main (failure)` while other sessions push, and the same commit is pushed again with the same lease at most twice more, after fixed waits of 1 and then 2 seconds with no override, these pushes not counting as attempts. Still the base after the last is exit 2 with git's message, including the remote's lines (the push drops `--quiet` if it hides them), and a line saying the failure may be transient and that running `clm land <branch>` again is safe. When the refetch fails the push may have gone through, so it exits 2 with the push's message alone. Ten moved attempts exit 1 saying `main` keeps moving.
7. Append, in one commit (`ledger/append-all`), the `effort` records `clm effort record --branch <branch>` would append for the branch's sessions on this machine, read once before the first attempt so neither the reading nor a second ledger push lengthens the time `main` has moved with no `landed` record (`effort-accounted`; the reader's warnings print prefixed `effort: `, and when it can't read at all it warns `effort not recorded: <why>`), then `landed`: commit, branch, `land_kind` and `review` from the gate's first line, fingerprint, the step-5 code key as `code_key`, the change IDs. When `append` reports `landed` unpushed, it warns `the landed record isn't on origin yet: git push origin clm/ledger/<person>/<clone>` and still exits 0, since `main` has moved. Then `host`'s `branch delete <branch> --lease <head>` for an `impl/*`, `spec/*` or `propose/*` branch (a failure warns), the local branch too when it is checked out nowhere (`clm wrap-up` tidies one that is), and for `impl/*`, **Freeing a build's disk** (below).
8. Print `landed <sha> <kind>`.

**Derived specs** (`derived-specs-fingerprint`). When the head adds a change (`clm-gate`'s `added_changes` over the merge base's and the head's `ls-tree -r -z` listings, the question `gate.fingerprint/fingerprint` asks), or applies one (a change whose `change.md` parses with status `proposed` or `approved` at the base, `origin/main`'s, and in the merge commit has status `applied`, or is absent while `changes/archive/<id>/change.md` has status `done`, as `sync`'s Design defines it: `regenerate-applied-live-changes`), the IDs of the added changes whose `change.md` in the merge commit parses with status `applied` (live) or `done` (new archived), and of the applied ones, sorted, go to `sync.regenerate`'s `regenerated` (crate `clm-regenerate`, which reaches no `clm-health`: `clm/health-isolated`) without its stale-delta merge (`sync.regenerate/regenerated-tree`), run with the tree holding the merge commit as `root` and the fetched base's commit SHA as `onto`, so the scratch `specs/`, the copied `changes/` and `backlog/` and the re-apply are `clm rebase`'s own. A stop exits 1 with rule `land/derived-specs`, the lines it returned and `clm rebase`; otherwise the regenerated `specs/` and the merge commit's are compared byte for byte, a symlink by its target, over the union of their paths in byte order, a path on one side only being a difference, and the first difference exits 1 naming it. It runs before step 5, so before the `--no-detectors` run too. The fingerprint skips `specs/` on a head that adds a change, so this is what keeps an approval from covering a hand edit there. A head that applies a change and adds none keeps `specs/` in its fingerprint, which reads listings alone and can't see a status, so a sync that regenerates its `specs/` onto a `main` that changed them asks for its approval again.

**Behind in `specs/`** (`land-regenerates-behind-specs`, `regenerate-dont-merge`). A head that adds or applies a change, as **Derived specs** reads them, is *behind in `specs/`* when a file under `specs/` changed both between the merge base and the base and between the merge base and the head, and every path `git merge-tree --write-tree --name-only origin/main <head>` reports conflicted, if any, lies under `specs/`. Concurrent spec lands make this the usual case: each regenerates the same `specs/<component>/SPEC.md` and the root `specs/SPEC.md`, and a land whose check runs for minutes can find `main` moved by another's when it pushes. Such a head lands without a `clm rebase` round, its next attempt regenerating again, in two regenerates. Whether a head is behind is decided where the attempt computes the merged tree; the code key and the lock decision use the tree merge-tree wrote, since the code key leaves `specs/` out, and both regenerates run in the attempt after that decision. The merge commit's message takes the `Change: <id>` line, since such a head adds or applies a change and so lands as `spec`. First the land proves the head's own `specs/` derived: `regenerated` from the tree on the head as `root`, onto the merge base, for the changes the head adds whose status there is `applied` or `done` and those it applies over the merge base, compared with the head's `specs/` as **Derived specs** compares; a stop or a difference exits 1 with rule `land/derived-specs`, the path or the lines, and `clm rebase`, so a hand edit under `specs/` is refused here as there. Then it regenerates as **Derived specs** does, onto the base, from a commit of the tree merge-tree wrote (whose `specs/` `regenerated` doesn't read, so conflict markers there don't matter), and the merge commit's tree is that tree with `specs/` replaced by the regenerated one; a stop, such as a delta stale against the base, exits 1 as **Derived specs** does, naming `clm rebase`. **Derived specs**' comparison then holds by construction and isn't run. Step 2's gate runs on that merge commit, which contains the base, so `gate/up-to-date` can't fire, and step 5's gate runs on it again as on any land. A conflict outside `specs/`, or a head that adds and applies no change (an `impl/*` branch's status edit), is still refused as step 3 says. The approval stands: the fingerprint skips `specs/` on a head that adds a change, and on one that only applies a change it holds the head's `specs/`, which the first regenerate has proved derived. What lands under `specs/` differs from the head's only by what the base brought, regenerated as `clm rebase` would have made it.

**Places** (`scratch-outside-git`, `land-tree`, `fixed-land-tree`). Build tools such as Cargo name a workspace's outputs by its path and judge them fresh by file times, so two lands that overlap on one build place can check each other's code, and a fresh path per land rebuilt everything (43 GB a day on one machine). The land hands its check that place as `CLM_LAND_DIR` and the project's check script points its toolchain there (the starter script does for cargo: `init/starter-check-land-dir`); the land sets no toolchain's variables itself. Before step 2 an attempt computes the merged tree and its code key (`git merge-tree --write-tree` writes only objects); when it will run `[land] check`, it takes the exclusive advisory lock on `<home>/.clm/land.lock` (`land-target-lock`, `<home>/.clm/` created when missing, released by the system when a land is killed), printing `waiting for another land's check on <home>/.clm/land: <n> ahead` on stderr when it begins to wait and again each time `<n>` changes, in the order **The queue** (below) sets, with no time limit, and holds it until the push has gone through and the cap is applied, or the attempt ends; the append, the deletes and the print run outside it. **The cap** (`land-target-cap`): right after a push that went through, still under the lock, it sums the lengths of the regular files under `.clm/land` (no symlink followed, so a sparse file counts its length) and, above `[land] target_cap_gb` gigabytes, removes the directory and prints `cleared .clm/land (<n> GB, cap <cap> GB)`; an unreadable entry or a failed removal only warns. Incremental data and units for feature sets no land builds again are what grow there, and Cargo prunes neither, so the cap trades one cold land for a bounded directory, and each attempt takes it afresh. A merge that conflicts and a checked code key whose readers select nothing take no land lock; a caller's `CARGO_TARGET_DIR` changes nothing here, since the land neither reads nor sets it. **The check lock** (`check-lock`): whatever the target, the run of `[land] check` itself, by a land or by a `--check-only` run whose `CLM_SLOT` (`check-slot`) is 0, holds a second exclusive advisory lock, on `<home>/.clm/check.lock`, from just before the command is spawned until it exits, taken with the same helper as the land lock (`lock.rs`, generalised to take the lock file's path and the waiting line) and printing exactly `waiting for another check on <home>/.clm/check.lock: <n> ahead` on stderr when it begins to wait and again each time `<n>` changes, in the order **The queue** sets, with no time limit; a lock file that can't be created or locked is exit 2, as the land lock's is. The lock is taken inside the helper both callers share for running `[land] check`, before the output pipe is read, and dropped when it returns, on every path. The project's tests share the host, not only the target: a test that binds a fixed local port fails in whichever of two overlapping checks starts second, for a reason that isn't its branch's. A `--check-only` run in a build place, whose slot isn't 0, takes no lock: its slot keeps its tests apart from every other run's (`tests-run-concurrently`), and builds' pre-land checks, the clone's most frequent checks, queued behind every land's while it took one (`worktree-check-counts`); runs outside a place share slot 0, which keeps nothing apart, so they still take it. The helper is told whether to take the lock; `verify`'s runner (`shared-check-runner`) always takes it. A land takes it after `land.lock`, never before, and `--check-only` takes at most it, so neither can wait on the other in a cycle; `clm affected`, `clm check` and the gate run outside it. A locked attempt checks out in the land tree `<home>/.clm/land-tree` (checks of a root in a subdirectory run in `<home>/.clm/land-tree/<prefix>`): when `git worktree list --porcelain` lists it (both canonicalised) and it exists, `git checkout --quiet --force --detach <commit>` and then `git clean --quiet -fdx`; otherwise, or when either fails, it removes the path, runs `git worktree prune` and `git worktree add --quiet --detach <path> <commit>`, a failed add being exit 2. The tree stays when the attempt ends; `clm land` and `clm wrap-up` never remove it, and removing it by hand starts the next land cold. Any other attempt uses a fresh `land-*` worktree under `TMPDIR` (read through the injected environment lookup) or else `std::env::temp_dir()`, removed on every exit path, never one under `.claude/worktrees/`, and outside the repository, so no tool that refuses paths under `.git/` (Vite's `server.fs.deny`) or walks up to project files trips on it; a killed land's tree stays until the system clears it.

**The queue** (`lock-queue`, `ordered-lock-queue`). The operating system wakes the waiters of an advisory lock in no stated order, so `lock.rs`'s helper puts an ordered queue in front of each lock and keeps the lock itself for exclusion. Each lock `<home>/.clm/<name>.lock` has its queue in the directory `<home>/.clm/<name>.queue/`. A run that finds the lock free and no live ticket in the queue takes the lock at once, printing nothing, with no ticket. Otherwise it takes a ticket: under a short exclusive lock on `<name>.queue/next.lock`, it reads the counter in `<name>.queue/next` (0 when missing), writes it back one higher, creates the ticket file `<counter, zero-padded to 20 digits>-<lane>-p<n>` (`lane` being `priority` or `ordinary`, `p<n>` the run's priority, `p1` to `p5`), takes an exclusive advisory lock on that file, and only then releases `next.lock`. The ticket's own lock is what makes it live: the system drops it when the run's process dies, however it dies, and a run that reads the queue, under `next.lock`, removes each ticket whose lock it can take, so a killed land never holds a turn and no process ID is read. **Turns.** Among the live tickets the turn goes, first, to the `p1` tickets, a `priority` one before an `ordinary` one and otherwise the oldest; then to the `priority` tickets, the highest priority first and then the oldest; then to the `ordinary` tickets in the same order; except that when the queue's `streak` file (in the same directory, written under `next.lock`) says the last six runs to take the lock below `p1` held `priority` tickets while an `ordinary` ticket was waiting, and no `p1` ticket waits, the `ordinary` ticket that would come first among them takes it. A waiter re-reads the queue at most every 250 milliseconds; only while the turn is its own does it try the lock, without blocking, and once it holds the lock it updates `streak` (unchanged when its ticket is `p1`, one higher when its ticket was `priority` and an `ordinary` ticket is waiting, 0 otherwise) and writes its ticket's name to the queue's `holder` file, both under `next.lock`, keeping the ticket and its lock until it releases the lock, when it removes the ticket. A run that takes the lock with no ticket writes `holder` empty and `streak` 0. A ticket is a file in the queue named by the pattern `<20 digits>-priority-p<1-5>` or `<20 digits>-ordinary-p<1-5>`; `next`, `next.lock`, `streak` and `holder` stay from run to run. `<n>`, in the waiting line, is the number of live tickets held or whose turn comes before the waiter's own, plus one when the lock is held and no live ticket is the one `holder` names; between a holder taking the lock and writing `holder` the count can be one too high for a moment, which only the line shows. **Lanes.** A land takes a `priority` ticket, for `land.lock` and for `check.lock` alike, when its merged tree's code key counts as checked (with the same command, or through the settings-free key) and **Readers of checked code** selected a test, so its check runs only those readers (a readers' selection that failed, so that every test runs, is `ordinary`); every other run, a land of unchecked code, a `--check-only` run with slot 0 and `verify`'s runner (`shared-check-runner`), takes an `ordinary` one. The lane is a parameter of `lock.rs`'s helper and of the crate's internal runner (`run_in`); the library call `run_check` (`shared-check-runner`) always waits with an `ordinary` ticket, so `verify` passes nothing new. A land knows its lane only where it takes the lock for its readers, after **Readers of checked code** has run `clm affected --readers`; its earlier `land.lock` wait, for unchecked code, is always `ordinary`. A land that waited there and, on preparing again, finds its code checked, releases that lock and its ticket and queues afresh with the lane its readers give it, so a land waits with the lane of the check it will run. **Priority.** A land's priority is `orchestrator`'s `land_priority` for its branch, head and the fetched base (`orchestrator/land-priority`), computed once per attempt before its first wait and kept when the land queues afresh within that attempt; every other run, a `--check-only` run and `verify`'s runner, waits at `p5`. A priority that can't be computed, such as specs that don't load at the base, is `p5` with a warning, since the order is never worth failing a land for. Exclusion never rests on the queue: a run that skips it, such as an older `clm`, still waits on the lock itself, and only the order is lost. A queue file that can't be created, read or locked is exit 2, as the lock's is.

**Its own binary** (`land-binary-copy`). A check that rebuilds `clm` can replace the file `clm land` runs from. So before its first subcommand it copies `std::env::current_exe` into a fresh `land-bin-*` directory beside its temporary worktrees, keeping permissions, and runs the gate, `clm affected` and `clm check` from the copy, removed on every exit path; when the copy can't be made it exits 1 naming the path. The lander's binary is `main`'s rule, which the merged tree lives under. `CLM_GATE`, `CLM_CHECK` and `CLM_AFFECTED` override the three commands, run as given, so tests fake them.

**Selected tests** (`graph-test-selection`). Before `[land] check` it runs `clm affected --base <sha>`, `<sha>` the fetched base's commit (the merge commit's merge base with it, so the selection is what the branch brings), in the tree on the merge commit and under the land lock when the check holds it (the check lock covers `[land] check` alone), and writes its stdout unchanged to `selection` in a temporary directory, which `CLM_SELECTION` names. When it fails, `clm affected failed: <the first non-empty line of its stderr, or the spawn error>; the check runs every test`, and the check runs without `CLM_SELECTION`: a failed selection costs time, never a skipped test.

**Readers of checked code** (`doc-readers-marked`). `code_key` leaves the doc-only paths out, so a `checked` record says nothing of the tests that read them: a spec branch's prose can fail such a test (a retired term under `specs/`) on a tree whose code was checked. A land whose code key is checked checks the merge commit out into a temporary worktree, as an attempt with no lock does, and runs `clm affected --base <sha> --readers` there (`check.affected/affected-readers`), `<sha>` as in **Selected tests**. When it prints nothing, the attempt goes on in that worktree with no lock. The gate on the head and **Derived specs** have run by then, in that worktree. When it prints a line, the attempt removes that worktree and goes on as one whose check runs: it takes the lock, checks the merge commit out into the land tree, so the check reuses the warm build place, and runs `[land] check`, so the readers' tests alone, with `CLM_SELECTION` naming a file holding that output unchanged. When it fails, it prints `clm affected failed: <reason>; the check runs every test` as **Selected tests** does and goes on the same way with `CLM_SELECTION` unset. A failing check exits 1 as any land's does.

**The check's output** (`land-check-log`, `check-step-markers`). One pipe (`std::io::pipe`) carries `[land] check`'s stdout and stderr in the order written, copied to stderr and to `<home>/.clm/land-logs/<branch>.log`, the branch's slashes as directories, created and truncated first; the last 40 lines (split on `\n`) are kept in memory. It reads until the pipe closes, so a check must end what it starts. **Steps.** While reading, the runner numbers the output's lines from 1 and follows the steps the check marks in GitHub Actions' form, testing each line with a trailing `\r` removed: a line starting `::group::` starts a step named by the rest of the line, trimmed, at that line, ending any step open at the line before it; a line that is `::endgroup::` once trailing whitespace is trimmed ends the open step at that line; a line of either kind can sit anywhere in the output, a marker split across reads is matched once the line is whole, and a last line with no `\n` counts. The step open when the pipe closes, if any, is the *last step*, spanning its marker line to the output's last line; when every step had ended, or none was marked, there is none. Only the name, the first line and the line count are kept, so the steps cost no memory beyond the tail. A failing check's refusal, printed last as `clm land: <why>`, is `last 40 lines of `<check>`:`, those lines and `` `<check>` fails on the merge with main ``, followed with a last step by `` ; the step open when it ended is `<name>`, lines <first> to <last> of its output `` and with a log by `` ; its output is at <path> ``, and the log stays; a passing check removes it. A log that can't be created warns naming the path, and the refusal then has no `; its output is at` clause; the line numbers then count the output copied to stderr. `clm check`'s output isn't logged, and `--check-only` names no step; `verify` prints the step and the tail after its outcome line (`verify/verify-run`).

**Step times** (`step-times`, `check-time-report`, `land-times-on-the-landed-record`). The runner that follows the check's steps also reads the clock, and `CheckRun` gains the timing beside its verdict, leaving `Step` as it is: a step's time runs from the read of its `::group::` line to the read of the line that ends it, or to the pipe's close for the last step, and the command's time from its start to its exit, each in whole seconds rounded down. Within a step, a line starting `::clm-selected::`, trailing whitespace trimmed, adds the rest of the line to the step's `selected_by`, each path once in the order first seen; such a line outside any step is ignored (`check-selector-lines`). A name may repeat, each step its own entry. Any attempt whose check command ran, a full selection or only the readers of checked code, puts the timing on the `landed` record it appends; a land that runs only `clm check` puts none. `--check-only` and `verify` use the same runner and record no timing. Once the check passes, and while the merged checkout and the land lock are still held, the land computes the chains it may print: for each of the up to three steps with the most seconds (ties in the check's order), `clm affected --why <path> --base <sha>` for the step's first `selected_by` path, run as it runs `clm affected`, against the same base. After step 8's append it prints on stderr `check <n>s, target <t>s`. When `<n>` is greater than `<t>` it then prints those steps, each as `  <name> <n>s` with its chain's lines indented below it, `  <name> <n>s, selected by no file` when it named no path, or `(no chain: <why>)` when the command failed; then `last <k> timed lands: <m> over target; most time in <step> <n>s, ...`, over this land and the last 19 timed lands `Fold::timed_lands(19)` gave in step 1's fold, naming the up to three steps with the most total seconds across them; then `see clm guide ci-and-deployment, where slow tests go`. None of it changes the land's outcome or exit status, and it files nothing: whether a pattern of slow lands calls for a backlog item, a selection fix or a move to `[verify]` is the reader's judgement, which the guide's section informs.

**Freeing a build's disk** (`land-frees-build-target`). After the push and the append of an `impl/*` land it deletes the `target/` at `git rev-parse --show-toplevel` when that is a linked worktree whose `HEAD` is the landed branch, printing `removed <path>`; a missing one is skipped and a failed delete warns. `clm run --finish` removes the worktree itself (`orchestrator.run`).

**`--check-only`** (`land-check-command`, `worktree-check-counts`). Given a branch as well it exits 1 (by hand: clap's `conflicts_with` would exit 2) and runs nothing. On any branch, in the CLM root (`--root`, else found from the current directory), it runs `[land] check` as step 5 does but with `CLM_LAND_DIR` removed (`land-dir-env`), so a build's check compiles where the caller's environment says, its own `target/` by default, then `clm check` with its detectors (`CLM_CHECK` overrides it), on the working tree as it stands, output on stderr. **The record.** Before the check command starts it reads `HEAD`'s commit (`git rev-parse HEAD`) and whether the working tree is clean: `git status --porcelain` in the CLM root's worktree prints nothing, so no tracked file differs from `HEAD`, staged or not, and there is no untracked file git doesn't ignore. After `clm check` passes it reads both again. When the tree was clean both times, `HEAD` was the same commit both times, and the local `origin/main` exists and is an ancestor of `HEAD` (`git merge-base --is-ancestor`), it appends, with `ledger`'s `append`, a `checked` record for `code_key(root, HEAD)` with the command a land's `checked` record names (`[land] check`, or `clm check` when none is set) and prints `recorded <HEAD's full sha> as checked`. Otherwise it appends nothing and prints `not recorded: <why>`, `<why>` being the first that holds of `the working tree has uncommitted changes`, `HEAD moved during the check` and `HEAD doesn't contain origin/main`. An append that fails warns `checked not recorded: <error>` and still passes, since a land then runs its own check; an append reported unpushed is enough, since a land on this clone folds the local writer's branch. It prints `checks pass` last; otherwise exits 1 with `` `<command>` fails `` or `` `clm check` fails ``, the failure messages being a parameter of the helpers a land shares, recording nothing; a `clm.toml` that doesn't load is exit 1 with `config`'s message. Its check command runs under `<home>/.clm/check.lock` (**The check lock**, above) only when its slot is 0, `<home>` found from the main worktree as a land finds it. **Why the record holds for the land** (`worktree-check-counts`). A land looks a `checked` record up by the merged tree's code key alone, an exact match, never by who wrote it or where (`legacy-code-key`). A land of a commit that contains the `main` it fetches makes a merge whose tree is that commit's tree, so its code key is the one recorded; when `main` has moved, the merged tree's code key differs whenever the code differs, and the land runs its usual selected check, the safety net. The record is made by the same `clm` helpers, on the same clone and machine and into the same ledger as a land's, from a clean committed tree that is the commit's own, with every test where a land would run only those `clm affected` selects, so it proves more than a land's record does; the build's full run stays the safeguard `graph-test-selection` counts on for a test the selection would skip. What differs is ignored state only: a build's own `target/`, where a land checks in a tree `git clean -fdx` has emptied of ignored files beside the warm `.clm/land`. A check that passes only because of an ignored file it doesn't make itself is the project check's own defect, which the next land after `main` moves, or `clm verify`, shows. It exists for a build's pre-land check (`orchestrator.run`), which Claude Code's worktree isolation refused as a script path.

**Offline** (`no-local-land-with-remote`, `none-keeps-its-own-remote`). Every host lands through `origin`: with `host = "none"` it is the clone's own remote (`host/none-keeps-a-remote`), so there is no local land and no `(local)` output. `main` has one copy, the remote's, so a step-1 fetch that fails for want of `origin` exits 1 with `offline: origin can't be reached; push and land when it can`. **A fetch that fails is tried again** (`offline-land`): lands from linked worktrees of one clone share its remote-tracking refs, and one land's ledger push or branch deletion can move `refs/remotes/origin/...` between another's fetch reading a ref and updating it, which git refuses with `cannot lock ref ... is at <x> but expected <y>`. So `Repo::fetch` runs `git fetch origin` up to three times, waiting 1 second and then 2 (the waits `land-push-retry` uses), and after the last failure runs `git ls-remote origin refs/heads/main`: when that fails too, `origin` can't be reached and the land is offline; when it succeeds, the land exits 1 with the last fetch's git message instead. `clm reconnect`'s fetch goes the same way. A truly offline land, or reconnect, spends the 3 seconds of waits before it says so. One helper on `Repo` does this for both, taking the fetch's arguments (`clm reconnect` passes `--quiet --prune`), with `ls-remote` run through `Repo::git` like every other git call (`git-plumbing-layer`) and the waits in the constant `push_main` uses, shared rather than copied; the refusal is `Error::Refused` holding the last fetch's `clm_git` error text, and `OFFLINE` stays in `lib.rs`. `clm approve`'s own fetch (`land.approval`) is left as it is (`backlog/clm-approve-calls-a-lost-ref-race-offline`). **`clm reconnect`**, for a repository whose `origin` is newly set, fetches `origin` with `--prune`, so a remote-tracking ref the old remote left doesn't stand for the new one's branch, then pushes `main` with a lease when `origin/main` is missing or an ancestor, then every local `clm/ledger/*` branch, and prints `reconnected <n> lands`; when `origin/main` has moved it pushes only the ledgers, prints `land again <branch>` for each local land (the merge commit's second parent) and exits 1.

**The push turn** (`push-turn`, `a-passed-check-is-kept`). A third advisory lock, `<home>/.clm/push.lock`, `<home>/.clm/` created when missing since a project with no `[land] check` has none, taken with `lock.rs`'s helper and a queue of its own beside it, every ticket `ordinary` at `P5` so turns go in arrival order: `P1` goes first on the land lock, and is left out here because a turn lasts seconds. An attempt that runs no `[land] check` takes it before step 1's fetch and holds it through the push. An attempt that ran one takes it once the check has passed, still holding the land lock, and fetches `main` again inside it: unchanged, it pushes; moved, it applies **A kept check**. The locks are always taken in the order land lock, push turn, check lock, so none waits on another in a cycle, and an attempt that holds the land lock never asks for it again. A land that waited for the land lock prepares again once it holds it on both of its paths, the early one for unchecked code and the late one for readers of checked code, so neither checks a merge made before the wait. A `clm` older than this rule takes no push turn, so its lands can still move `main` under a turn; the attempt then goes on as for a push from another clone.

**A kept check** (`overlap-recheck`, `a-passed-check-is-kept`). The attempt keeps the merge commit `P` its checks passed on. Inside the push turn, with `main` now at `M`, it runs `clm affected --overlap <P> <M>` in a temporary worktree (`P` holds the earlier `main`, so their merge base is that `main`), rebuilds its merge commit on `M`, runs the gate and `clm check` on it, runs `[land] check` with the overlap's selection in the land tree when the overlap printed a line, and pushes. A conflict while rebuilding, an `M` whose code key no `checked` record names, or an `--overlap` that fails ends the attempt as a moved `main` does, and the next attempt starts from step 1. Such rounds count toward the ten attempts. Lands taken together keep their check the same way, `P` being the last of their merge commits.

**Lands taken together** (`batched-lands`). A ticket for the land lock holds its land's branch name as the file's content, written before the ticket is locked and before the waiting line prints, its name unchanged, so a `clm` without this rule still reads the queue's order and its own empty tickets are left to wait. The attempt that takes the lock reads the waiting tickets in `turn_order`, resolves each branch in the clone's own refs, and builds the chain: for each, the approval and gate checks of steps 1 and 2, `git merge-tree` onto the chain's tip, a merge commit whose first parent is the commit before it, and the gate on that commit. The tip's code key is computed from the trees before any commit is made, and every commit of a chain of more than one holds the line `Checked: <that key>` in its message, after `Change: <id>` when it has one. Each member's part of the selection is what step 5's paths give it alone, with `clm affected --overlap` for it and each other member; the check runs their union. One `checked` record for the tip's key is appended before the push, as now; after it, one `append_all` holds every member's `effort` and `landed` records, written and signed by the clone's one writer as the holder's own are. A waiting land wakes when the holder's ticket is gone, prepares again, and finds its `landed` record in the fold.

**A failed batch** (`failed-batch`). The suspects are found from the failed run's output and the members' parts of the selection, with no further check. A suspect left to land alone has `alone` written after the branch name in its ticket, which the next holder reads: it neither takes that ticket nor, when the ticket's own land takes the lock, takes others with it. The mark lasts for that one attempt. Halving keeps the first `ceil(n/2)` suspects in queue order.

**Finishing an unrecorded land** (`unrecorded-land-finished`). Step 1, before the gate, the lock and the merge, looks for a commit on `origin/main`'s first-parent chain whose second parent is the head. A head on that chain itself, or one whose merge a `landed` record names, is not this rule's and lands as before. With no `landed` record for the commit, it is covered by its own code key's `checked` record, or by the `checked` record for the key its `Checked:` line names when that key is the code key of the last commit of the run of first-parent commits holding the same line; the other commits of that run are the push's other lands. The fingerprint is recomputed from the commit's parents as `ledger`'s verify recomputes it.
