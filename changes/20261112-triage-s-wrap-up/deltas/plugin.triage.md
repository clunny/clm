## MODIFIED Design

`triage` works the backlog down (`backlog-triage`), unattended once the human has picked what to work. Before its first draft it tells the human once that drafts run under the session's permission mode, as `build` does for builds, and never refuses a mode. It runs `clm triage` and shows its lines, then the menu (`triage-menu`), and works only the choices the human picks, in that order, each line once, so approvals land first and answers are recorded before the drafts that carry them launch. A `designed` line is put to the human only through the menu's *Approvals*. A `human` line is put to the human one at a time, with a recommendation, any answer that splits a component being offered only for a cut `clm survey --components` accepts (`triage-checks-splits`), and drafted once answered (`triage-answers`). An idea is brainstormed with the human in this session, one at a time while the drafts run, never drafted (`ideas-brainstormed`). The `open` and `main` lines are other work, still being designed or waiting to be built and possibly another session's, so they are never put to the human for approval: the summary lists them.

It takes the picked `group` lines in order and, while the clone has a free place (`build-places`), runs `clm draft <slug>...` with the next line's items and, when that exits 0, passes its stdout unchanged to the Agent tool as a background `general-purpose` subagent with `isolation: "worktree"`, one per group, in parallel, keeping the change ID from the `draft <id>` line `clm draft` prints last on stderr; it never designs in its own context (`triage-caps-drafts`). When `clm draft` exits 3, no place is free: the group waits, and the skill launches it, and then the groups after it in order, as drafts return and free places. When `clm draft` exits 1 or 2 it tells the human what it printed and launches nothing for that group. Whenever a subagent returns, whatever its closing line, it runs `clm draft --finish <id>` with that draft's ID, which frees its place and removes the draft's worktree once its change has landed, and then launches the next waiting group. Then it reads the closing line: `landed <sha>`, a change that needed no review, is noted; `proposed <id> review`, one that needs review, joins the fold list; `stopped: <reason>` is told to the human, and the group isn't retried, since a draft that pushed its branch shows as `open` on the next `clm triage` and `/clm:propose` resumes it. Each `left <slug>: <reason>` line before the closing line is kept for the summary.

When every subagent has returned and the fold list isn't empty, it folds them (`yes-no-review`): it runs `clm draft --fold <id>...` with the fold list's IDs in the order of the `group` lines they were drafted from, which is `clm triage`'s order, and, when that exits 0, passes its stdout unchanged to the Agent tool as one background `general-purpose` subagent with `isolation: "worktree"`; when it exits non-zero it tells the human what it printed and lists those branches in the summary. A rename change folds alone (`fold-rename-alone`): for each `left <id>:` line saying so, whatever the first fold's exit, it runs `clm draft --fold <id>` with that ID alone and launches its fold the same way, so a rename change that needs review reaches the same one ask; such a change is neither a branch not folded nor a change the fold left (`triage-folds-renames-alone`). The fold's closing line is `proposed <batch> review <url>` or `stopped: <reason>`, after a `left <id>: <reason>` line for each change it couldn't fold without the human, whose branch stays, with no pull request, for `/clm:propose`. On `proposed <batch> review <url>` it asks the human once, in the session (`approve-in-session`), and on a yes lands the branch as `propose` does, in the worktree that holds `spec/<batch>` (`git worktree list` shows it: the fold's, or with one change the draft's). Once it has landed, `clm draft --finish <id>` for each folded draft removes its worktree and deletes its branches now that its change is on `main`; then, when `<batch>` isn't one of their IDs, `clm draft --finish <batch>` runs from the session's own checkout, not the fold's worktree (which `--finish` skips when run inside it), and removes the fold's own worktree and deletes its local branch. On a plain no it leaves the branch. On a no that comes with a different design, an answer the human gives in the session, it revises the fold as `propose` revises a declined change, in the worktree that holds `spec/<batch>` (`triage-revises-declined-fold`): it edits the declined change there, re-applies it with `clm rebase`, pushes with `--force-with-lease` and asks again. That is the one design triage does in its own context, since the human is in the conversation; `clm draft` can't take the item again, because the fold's branch still proposes it. A `stopped` fold is told to the human and not retried: the drafts' branches still hold their work and show as `open` on the next `clm triage`. Then it runs `clm wrap-up`, and archives the changes it lists `complete` as `build` does (`triage-archives-complete`): on one branch `impl/archive/<id>` cut from `origin/main`, where `clm rebase` archives every complete change and deletes the items they resolve (`archive-branches`, `archive-on-sync`), landed with `clm land`, never by editing a change's `status` or running `clm archive`. It ends with a summary: what landed, the fold's branch with its pull request when it is left open, the changes the fold left, the `open` lines, the `human` lines with the requirements they block, the ideas brainstormed and how each ended, the menu's choices not picked with their counts, the items drafts left with their reasons, and the changes archived or the archive branch left to another session; and, once the checkpoint exits 0, the line that everything is saved and the session can be ended or cleared. `propose` designs one intent with the human in the conversation, and resumes a draft that stopped.

`triage` checks a split it recommends to the human the same way (`triage-checks-splits`).

**Tests** (`tests-count-where-cited`). The tests of this component's requirements sit in `crates/clm/tests/plugin_triage.rs`, a test file of its own beside `plugin.rs`: its build moves each test that cites one of its requirements out of `plugin.rs` into that file, with copies of the helpers those tests use and the `clm:reads` markers for the files they read (`doc-readers-marked`), so its code count holds its own tests rather than the whole of `plugin.rs`. `plugin`'s tests of the shared rules read this component's file too; a build that changes the file keeps them passing.

## ADDED Requirements

### Requirement: triage-archives-complete
`skills/triage/SKILL.md` SHALL tell Claude, when `clm wrap-up` prints `complete <id>` lines, to archive those changes on one code branch `impl/archive/<id>`, `<id>` the first change it lists `complete`, cut from `origin/main`, where `clm rebase` archives every complete change and deletes the items they resolve (`archive-branches`, `archive-on-sync`), as `build`'s wrap-up does, and never by setting a change's `status` by hand or running `clm archive`. It SHALL tell Claude:
- first to remove a leftover `.claude/worktrees/archive` whose branch has landed, with `git worktree remove .claude/worktrees/archive`, and, when another session's `impl/archive/<id>` is still unlanded on `origin`, to leave archiving to that branch and say so in the summary;
- otherwise to cut the branch in a worktree of its own with `git worktree add .claude/worktrees/archive -b impl/archive/<id> origin/main` and switch the session into it with the EnterWorktree tool's `path`, as it switches into a fold's worktree (`triage-enters-branch-worktree`), or, in a session isolated under `.claude/worktrees/` (`session-worktrees`), to cut it in its own checkout with `git switch -c impl/archive/<id> origin/main` and add no worktree;
- there to run `clm rebase` until its last line is `rebase ok`, push with `clm branch push`, open its pull request with `clm pr open impl/archive/<id>` and a body written under `.clm/`, and land it with `clm land impl/archive/<id>`; when the push is refused because `impl/archive/<id>` already exists on `origin`, to leave the archive to the session that pushed it;
- once the branch has landed or its push was refused, to switch back to its own checkout as it does after a fold's land and remove the worktree with `git worktree remove .claude/worktrees/archive`, never forcing it and saying so when it refuses, or, when isolated, to switch its checkout off the branch with `git switch --detach origin/main`; then to delete the local branch with `git branch -D` when its push was refused, and to run `clm wrap-up` again.

It SHALL NOT name a branch `impl/archive` without `/<id>`, which the queue would read as a claim on a component `archive`.

#### Scenario: triage archives complete changes
- GIVEN the plugin's `skills/triage/SKILL.md`
- WHEN a test reads it
- THEN it contains `complete <id>`, `impl/archive/<id>`, `git worktree add .claude/worktrees/archive -b impl/archive/<id> origin/main`, `git switch -c impl/archive/<id> origin/main`, `clm land impl/archive/<id>` and `git worktree remove .claude/worktrees/archive`

#### Scenario: triage with no archive step
- GIVEN a `triage` `SKILL.md` that doesn't mention `impl/archive/<id>`
- WHEN the test's check runs on it
- THEN it reports `impl/archive/<id>`

#### Scenario: triage archive branch read as a claim
- GIVEN a `triage` `SKILL.md` that names `impl/archive` followed by a space or a backtick
- WHEN the test's check runs on it
- THEN it reports `impl/archive/<id>`
